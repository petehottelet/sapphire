# Silhouette first, material second

Treat geometry and Sapphire styling as separate stages. The shape determines where material exists; the optical effect supplies surface shading, refraction, and highlights.

## 1. Obtain the geometry

- Existing mark: use the supplied vector geometry or clean source alpha. Preserve all intentional holes and separate components.
- Typography: typeset the exact text with the actual requested font and weight. Preserve kerning, tracking, line breaks, counters, and the natural aspect ratio. Follow the typography guide.
- New object or concept: use image generation to create a simple, clean silhouette before adding material. Request a flat opaque shape, clear negative space, transparent surroundings, and no gradients, glow, shadows, or optical effects. Inspect the shape for recognizability and unwanted detail.
- Illustration or layout: create masks for relevant forms and retain their arrangement and necessary internal detail. A silhouette stage does not authorize deleting the content of a requested illustration or diagram.

Use a mask or alpha representation appropriate to the available tools. Do not mistake a black image background for a transparent silhouette mask. Keep this clean geometry reference separately from the styled artwork.

## 2. Establish the material support

For a frameless graphic, the silhouette itself is the glass object. For text, apply glass to the letters and preserve transparent counters and inter-letter spaces. For an icon tile, the lens silhouette and white pictogram are separate shapes; use a tile only when the requested format calls for one.

Resolve the silhouette through internal inspection and proceed. Ask the user only when a substantial ambiguity in the subject or geometry cannot be inferred; do not insert a routine approval gate between these stages.

## 3. Generate the optical treatment

Use image generation/editing for the creative material. Supply the silhouette as geometry authority and a Sapphire reference as material authority. Request dark convex glass, compact caustics along relevant upper-right/lower-left contours, narrow cool/warm spectral edge separation, and quiet interiors. Preserve content and silhouette rather than inventing new boundaries.

Prompt recipe:

"Apply Sapphire material to the supplied silhouette. Its outline, holes, disconnected pieces, spacing, and aspect ratio are authoritative. Create dark convex optical glass with compact blue/cyan/white caustics, small warm prismatic tips, and restrained chromatic aberration contained at its edges. Keep transparent areas transparent. Apply the treatment to the shape itself; add no enclosing tile, backdrop, external shadow, new elements, or extra lettering."

## 4. Constrain and verify the result

Use a supported generation/editing mask or, for an authorized deterministic composition workflow, clip the generated optical material to the retained alpha mask or vector paths. Preserve the mask's antialiased edges and holes; place any required extra effect margin deliberately rather than silently growing the silhouette. Keep creative optical rendering in the image tool.

Compare final and reference geometry at full size and intended display size. Check spelling and counters for text, separate pieces for symbols, and layout for multi-element graphics. If the available tools cannot enforce the silhouette, make focused generative corrections and describe the remaining uncertainty. An image-generation prompt alone does not establish exact geometric preservation.

## 5. Export the artwork

Deliver the requested format with real alpha where requested. Inspect both outer padding and internal holes. Preserve the clean silhouette, styled master, and composition settings when the user requests reusable source or reproducibility. Use the export guide for contact sheets and file verification.
